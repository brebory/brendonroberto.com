Skills you sew

April 7, 2026

In most action RPGs you level up and pick skills from a tree. In Fiber, a skill is an item. You weave it from a pattern, embroider it with modifiers, and swap it like gear. These are emblems.

Weave, embroider, unstitch

Emblems reuse the crafting language from items and affixes, with fiber verbs:

  • Patterns are recipes you learn from bosses and quests. Each one lets you weave a specific emblem.
  • Weave an emblem from a pattern and materials. It comes out at a grade, Common through Legendary, like everything else.
  • Embroider an emblem with modifiers. It starts with one slot and can grow to three.
  • Unstitch a modifier to get it back intact. It’s free, for the same reason pulling affixes off items is free: experimenting shouldn’t cost you.

There’s also upgrading, rerolling, slot expansion and salvage, each built as its own small operation with its own tests.

What a modifier can do

Item affixes mostly change numbers on you. Emblem modifiers change how the skill behaves:

ModifierExample
Projectile count+2 projectiles
Area+30% area
Damage+10 damage, or +25%
Cost−20% stamina cost
Cost conversionPay with HP instead of MP
Cooldown−0.5 s cooldown

Skills can cost any mix of stamina, mana and health, which is what makes cost conversion interesting: a mana-hungry build can choose to spend health instead.

Under the hood, a skill definition describes the base skill: its type (projectile, melee, movement, buff and so on), element, costs, cooldown and attack data. When you equip an emblem, a resolver folds its modifiers over the definition and caches the final skill. Changing the modifiers clears the cache, so a skill isn’t recomputed every time you use it.

Skills are keys

Emblems double as Fiber’s metroidvania keys. Gates in the world ask for a skill with the right property, like an element, a kind of movement or enough force to break something, and you open them by equipping the right emblem and using it on the gate. Your build decides where you can explore, and the keys are things you crafted.

Managing them

Emblems live in the same inventory as items. Their cards share the item card’s layout, and there’s a management dialog to cycle through what you own, slot them into skill buttons, and embroider or unstitch.

Ember Slash

The first skill to get real visual feedback is Ember Slash, a fiery melee strike. A skill definition can now name an animation and an effect scene, the resolver carries them through, and the skill state plays the animation and spawns the effect when you use it. Ember Slash reuses the melee slash effect with a new fire tint shader on top, so a new elemental slash is mostly a tint and a data file.
